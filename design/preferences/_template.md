# PREF-NNNN — <short title>

**Polarity:** like | reject | conditional
**Facet terms:** `X.YYY.term`, `X.YYY.term`
**Scope:** where this applies (all work / product UI / brand graphics / a named project)
**Evidence:** what was said or shown, quoted if possible, and the date
**Date:** YYYY-MM-DD

**The preference.** One or two sentences. What to do, or what not to do.

**Why.** The reason behind it. Without this the rule gets misapplied the moment
circumstances change — which is the whole failure mode this database exists to prevent.

**Exceptions.** When it does *not* hold. Write "none observed" rather than leaving it blank.

**Related:** PREF-NNNN · DDR-NNNN · [[root-memory-slug]]

---

Rules for writing one of these:

- **Evidence or nothing.** A preference inferred from a guess is worse than an unknown,
  because it gets designed to. If there is no statement or rejection behind it, leave the
  facet cell `–` and do not create a record.
- **One preference per record.** If it needs "and", it is two records.
- **Record the rejection, not the replacement only.** What was turned down is the data; what
  was accepted instead is context.
- **Scope honestly.** A preference stated about a brand graphic is not evidence about
  product UI. Over-scoping is how a taste database becomes wrong.
