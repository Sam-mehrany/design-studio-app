# REF-0021 — Ferox — "Innovating Precision Tools For Your Industry" monochrome monitor hero

## Read

Values below are **estimated by eye** — pixel samples landed on the grey staging background, not the screen, so no hex is claimed as measured. One line of context: an industrial tools company's landing hero on a monitor — a greyscale photograph of a multi-spindle machine filling the left two-thirds, white headline on the right, black pill nav top-left.

**Colour.** Estimated by eye: the whole screen is greyscale — black header, mid-grey photo (about #8A8A8A), light-grey panel (about #C8C8C8). Monochrome with zero accent: hierarchy is carried entirely by tone steps and position, not by hue.

**Typography.** Estimated by eye: headline "Innovating Precision Tools For Your Industry" in a grotesque at about 24–28 px, nav at about 9–10 px, logo at about 11 px. Two-tier scale — the headline is the only display size, everything else is one small tier. No numerals on screen.

**Layout & harmony.** Split grid, estimated by eye: photograph left (~65%), headline and a round arrow button right (~35%), pill nav floating top-left, cart pill top-right. Hierarchy is ranked by position first (the machine fills the frame), then by the headline's size and white-on-grey weight. Density is low — one photo, one headline, one nav.

**Shape & surface.** Estimated radius scale, by eye: pill nav and pill cart button (about 12–14 px radius), the monitor itself sharp-cornered with a thin black frame. Separation is tonal — black header band against the grey photo — with no border and no shadow inside the screen.

**Buttons & components.** The components are the pill nav, the white pill "Cart (0)" with a cart icon, and a single round outline arrow button under the headline. All are quiet relative to the photograph; the arrow is the only explicit next step and it is small.

**Gradient & imagery.** No gradient. One full-bleed greyscale photograph of the machine is the hero: delete it and there is no page left, just a headline on grey — so the dependency is hard. The photograph is also the product evidence, which is the point for an industrial buyer.

**Contrast.** Estimated by eye: white headline on mid-grey photo is about 3:1 estimated — fails 4.5:1 where the headline crosses the lighter part of the machine image (estimated, not measured). White on the black nav pill is about 17:1 — passes. The small grey nav text on black is the quietest element and the most likely failure.

## Why the verdict

Adapt: the monochrome photo-led split hero with a pill nav is a transferable industrial-landing mechanism, but the composition collapses without its photograph (imagery_dep hard) and it is a staged concept mockup, so adapt, not adopt.
