# REF-0044 — Translucent concentric rings with construction lines, mid-century print

## Read

*All hex values and contrast ratios below are **measured** with Python/PIL off the image (region medians, median-cut clusters, or darkest/lightest-5% glyph samples; WCAG 2.x formula). Type sizes and ratios are estimated by eye.*

**Colour.** A sequential warm ramp built by stacking translucent rings: deep red `#a51d17` → coral `#c47852` → tan `#d9a982` → cream `#ebd8bb` / `#f4e5ca`, ending in an olive-grey core. Ground is aged paper `#ebd8bb`.

**Typography.** Tiny italic serif labels ("a.1", "b.1", "x", "y") as geometry annotations — mathematical-textbook register.

**Layout & harmony.** Vertical stack of ring systems on one axis; white dashed construction circles and lines overlay them, implying a geometric proof.

**Shape & surface.** Only circles and dashed hairlines; depth from overlapping transparency, not shadow. Paper grain over everything.

**Gradient & imagery.** No photography; the grain is a raster overlay that could be dropped.

**Contrast.** Label "a.1" `#59401f` on paper `#ebd8bb` = 6.92:1; "b.1" `#791104` on the coral ring `#d65c51` = 2.91:1 — **fails** (text). Deep red on cream = 5.41:1.

## Why the verdict

Vector geometry plus a sequential ramp: the stacked-transparency rings are a good model for a sequential data scale or progress rings; the paper grain and italic-serif annotations are finish, not mechanism.
