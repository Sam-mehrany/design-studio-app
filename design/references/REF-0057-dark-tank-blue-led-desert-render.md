# REF-0057 — Dark battle tank with blue LED strips and turret drone in desert dusk — 3D render

## Read

Values below were **measured by sampling pixels** (hex values from sampled points); there is no typography in the image. One line of context: a 3D render of a dark battle tank in a desert at dusk, with a small drone mounted on the turret — the only outdoor ground-vehicle shot and the only one with a drone. No UI archetype applies.

**Colour.** Measured: near-black hull and turret against a pale dusk sky (#FFFCF7 dominant, warm haze) and dusty ground. The single accent is a thin blue LED strip running the length of the hull — sampled in the blue range, the only saturated colour in the frame — with small red sensor lights as a secondary dot. Dark-first value strategy: the vehicle is a black silhouette first, the blue line second.

**Composition & form.** Three-quarter front view, cannon pointing left, tank centred and low, dust haze softening the ground line. The turret carries the visual incident: a quad-rotor drone perched on top, sensor pods and a launcher box breaking the turret's slab silhouette. The blue LED strip is the image's horizontal datum, running unbroken along the hull side and tying the whole vehicle together.

**Light & surface.** Low warm dusk sun from behind-left, long soft shadows, dust in the air catching the light — the only atmospheric render on the board. Surfaces are matte dark armour with scuffed tracks; the LED strip glows rather than reflects, the one light-emitting element in the image.

**Typography.** None — no legible type anywhere in the image.

**Contrast.** Measured: the black vehicle against the pale dusk sky is very high figure/ground separation, and the glowing blue strip against the black hull is the highest local contrast on the board. No text, so no WCAG pair applies.

## Why the verdict

Adapt: the blue-LED-as-datum-line is the transferable lesson — one thin glowing line organising a dark, complex form — and the dusk atmosphere is the strongest mood on the board, but a concept render, so adapt as mood/form reference only.
