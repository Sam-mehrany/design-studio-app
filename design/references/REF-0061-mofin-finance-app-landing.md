# REF-0061 — Mofin — finance app landing page, lavender gradient hero and dark pricing

## Read

Values below are **measured where stated** (hex values sampled from pixels); type sizes are estimated by eye and labelled as such. One line of context: a full-page finance-app landing capture — lavender-gradient hero with a hand holding a phone, feature bento, dark pricing section, testimonials and blog — with naira (₦) amounts throughout.

**Colour.** Measured: page field is near-white (#F6F6F6 dominant), hero is a soft lavender-pink gradient, pricing section is near-black (#222021 sampled). Single accent in practice: black, used for the primary buttons and the pricing block, against pastel tints (lavender, pale blue, pale mint) in the feature cards. The pastel tints categorise the bento cards rather than carrying meaning — decoration, not signal.

**Typography.** Estimated by eye: hero headline "Unlock Financial Clarity with Mofin" at about 48–56 px grotesque, tight leading, with "Mofin" in a lighter weight as the only weight play; section headlines at about 32–36 px; body at about 13–14 px. Dramatic scale — hero to body is roughly 4:1 — and the ₦20,983 / ₦29,880 figures inside the phone mockups are the only tabular numerals, set larger than their labels.

**Layout & harmony.** Single column, full-page capture: nav, hero (headline left, phone photo right), three-column features, bento grid ("Get the app", "Financial Analytics", "Smart Savings Goals", "Expense & Income Tracking"), four-step "How It Works", testimonial carousel, dark three-tier pricing, blog cards. The bento breaks the single-column rhythm once, in the middle, which is the page's one moment of spatial variety; everything else is stacked and centred.

**Shape & surface.** Estimated by eye: large radii throughout (about 16–24 px on cards, pill buttons), separation by tone (pastel card tints, black pricing block) with no visible shadows and hairline borders only on the pricing cards. One family of radii and one family of tint — the page is visually consistent even though the sections are many.

**Buttons & components.** Primary action is a black pill ("Get Started", "Download Free App"); secondary is an email field beside it in the hero. Pricing cards each end in a "Get Started" pill, white on the dark cards. The components are quiet relative to the headlines — the page sells with type and tint, not with loud controls.

**Gradient & imagery.** The hero gradient (lavender to pink, subtle, not mesh) sits behind the phone photo; the photo of the hand holding the phone is the hero's real image. Removing the photo would leave headline plus gradient — still readable, but the product would be abstract; soft dependency. Blog cards at the foot use photography as thumbnails.

**Contrast.** Measured: page field #F6F6F6 against black headlines is very high (about 18:1 for near-black on near-white). The pastel tints are too light to carry text and are not asked to — all text sits on white or black. No measured failing pair; small grey body copy is estimated by eye at about #6B7280 on white, roughly 4.8:1 — passes, estimated, not measured. Note a data oddity, not a contrast issue: pricing shows Pro and Premium both at $160.00/month — a concept-shot inconsistency.

## Why the verdict

Adapt: the pastel-tint bento plus dark pricing block is a calm, transferable landing structure at medium cost, but a concept capture with a pricing inconsistency and no live data, so adapt, not adopt.
