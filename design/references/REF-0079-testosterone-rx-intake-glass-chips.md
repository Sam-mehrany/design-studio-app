# REF-0079 — Testosterone RX intake — "Which areas of your health are you looking to improve?", glass chips

## Read

Values below: hex values **measured by pixel sampling** (dominant colours); type sizes estimated by eye and labelled as such. One line of context: frame 4 of 11 of a story pin — a hand holding a tilted phone showing a multi-select intake question ("Which areas of your health are you looking to improve?") with four frosted-glass option chips (Energy levels, Libido, Muscle, Mood), a "speak" voice button and a "Next" bar. Only this frame was analysed; the other 10 frames of the story were not supplied.

**Colour.** Measured: phone field blue-grey #7A96AB, light page #D9D9D9, black #010101, pale olive glow (estimated ~#BFB87B). Monochrome blue-grey throughout: white text on the blue-grey field, frosted chips in lighter/darker tints of the same hue, one olive-yellow glow behind the voice button. Colour means nothing here — the chips differ by blur and tone, not hue.

**Typography.** Estimated by eye: question ~30–34 px light, "Select all that apply" ~16 px, chip labels ~18 px, "Next" ~18 px. Dramatic scale with a light weight — the question is the whole screen, the chips are one quiet tier. The phone is tilted ~15°, so the type reads on a diagonal.

**Layout & harmony.** One tilted phone filling the frame, a hand holding it from below. Inside: a "Testosterone RX" pill top, the question upper third, a 2×2 grid of large frosted chips, the circular "speak" voice button overlapping the grid's lower right, a "Next" pill bar bottom. Hierarchy is size (question) then enclosure (chips). Space is generous; density sparse — four options and a voice path only.

**Shape & surface.** Radius scale estimated: chips ~28–32 px with hairline white borders, the voice button a ~90 px circle, the "Next" bar a long pill. Separation is by live blur: the chips are frosted glass over the blue-grey field, with a soft glow behind the voice button. This is the only pin in the batch using live blur, and it is the whole finish — remove the blur and the chips are flat translucent rectangles.

**Buttons & components.** The loudest element is the glowing "speak" voice button — a voice-input alternative to tapping chips, which is the screen's actual mechanism. The chips are multi-select (numbered 01–04, no visible selected state in this frame). The "Next" bar has a white circular arrow button at its left end.

**Gradient & imagery.** No gradient. The photograph of the hand holding the phone is the frame's context — a product photo, not a screenshot; delete it and the screen still reads, so dependency is soft, but the tilt and the hand are what make the glass readable. The frosted chips are a material treatment, not imagery.

**Contrast.** Computed from sampled colours: white #FFFFFF on blue-grey #7A96AB = 2.92:1 — fails 4.5:1 for body text and fails 3:1 for large text only marginally (2.92:1). The question text, chip labels and "Next" all sit on this field and all fail. This is the clearest contrast failure in the batch, and it is structural — the pale field cannot carry white text.

## Why the verdict

Adapt: the multi-select intake with a voice alternative is a genuinely good mechanism (tap or speak), but the glass finish sits on the active brief's reject line (E.DEP.live-blur), the cost is high, and white-on-blue-grey fails contrast at 2.92:1 measured — adapt, not adopt. The frame is 4/11 of a story; the other frames may show the selected state and the voice flow.
