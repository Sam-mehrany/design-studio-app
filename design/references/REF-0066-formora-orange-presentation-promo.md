# REF-0066 — FORMORA — orange presentation promo, "The most expensive mistake" furniture slides

## Read

Values below are **measured where stated** (hex values sampled from pixels); type sizes are estimated by eye and labelled as such. One line of context: a presentation-design promo showing three panels for a furniture brand "FORMORA" — a giant wordmark with an orange chair, then two versions of a "THE MOST EXPENSIVE MISTAKE" slide with statistics — on an orange textured background, with a Russian order-contact line at the top. A slide deck promo, not a website; J.ACQ.landing is the closest archetype, not an exact fit.

**Colour.** Measured: burnt-orange field sampled around #D34A20, with white panels, black text and a black slide at the bottom. Single accent — the orange itself — used as the field, the chair colour and the highlight; everything else is black and white. Dark-first in effect: the orange reads as dark against the white panels, and the black slide anchors the bottom. The orange is both background and product colour, which is the point — the furniture and the page are the same hue.

**Typography.** Estimated by eye: the "FORMORA" wordmark spans the full width at an estimated 90–110 px, grotesque, with the "O" replaced by an orange asterisk-like mark; the slide headline "THE MOST EXPENSIVE MISTAKE" is all-caps at about 28–32 px; statistics "75% / 25% / 90 min / 07 sec" are set at about 28–32 px with small captions. High-contrast weights — extrabold headlines against regular body — and dramatic scale: wordmark roughly 8× body. The statistics are the only numerals, and they are the slide's real content.

**Layout & harmony.** Three panels stacked with orange gutters: a white wordmark panel, an orange gradient slide, a black slide with two images. Each panel is its own grid, but the statistic row (75% / 25% / 90 / 07) repeats identically on the second and third panels — the same slide shown twice in two treatments, which makes the comparison the layout's idea: same content, orange version vs black version.

**Shape & surface.** Estimated by eye: large radii on the white panel (about 20 px), flat surfaces throughout, no shadows, separation by colour blocks alone. The orange chair in the first panel is a 3D render with soft volume; the rest of the page is flat. The asterisk "O" in the wordmark is the one graphic mark, repeated as a motif on the chair fabric in the third panel.

**Buttons & components.** There are no buttons; the components are the panels, the statistic row and the chair images. The loudest element is the wordmark, then the all-caps headline, then the statistics — a strict three-step hierarchy with nothing competing.

**Gradient & imagery.** The second panel uses an orange gradient (dark at the edges, lighter in the middle) behind the chair photo; the third panel uses photography (chair detail, white sculptural chair). Soft dependency: remove the chair images and the wordmark plus statistics still make the argument, but the product disappears. The gradient is decorative and sits on the brief's reject line only as mesh — this is a linear/radial wash, not mesh, so it is noted as gradient-subtle-adjacent, not flagged as mesh.

**Contrast.** Measured: burnt orange #D34A20 field against white panels is high separation as blocks; white text on the orange slide is moderate (orange is mid-value), and the slide keeps its body copy small and light — the headline carries the panel, the body is secondary. Black on white and white on black are both about 18:1 — pass. No measured failing text pair; the small captions on orange are the closest to the edge and are estimated, not measured.

## Why the verdict

Adapt: the same-slide-twice comparison (orange vs black treatment) and the wordmark-as-image are both strong presentation devices at medium cost, but this is a deck promo, not a website, so adapt, not adopt.
