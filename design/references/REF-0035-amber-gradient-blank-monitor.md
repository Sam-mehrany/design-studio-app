# REF-0035 — Amber gradient blank monitor on rock (no UI — gradient-only display)

## Read

Values below are **estimated by eye** — pixel samples landed on the dark staging rock, not the screen, so no hex is claimed as measured. One line of context: a monitor on a rock against an orange gradient wall, its screen showing only an abstract amber silk gradient — no type, no controls, no interface at all.

**Colour.** Estimated by eye: the screen runs from near-black (about #140604) through amber (about #B45A10) to a pale gold highlight (about #E8B25A) in a diagonal silk fold; the wall behind grades from pale sage (about #C8D2C4) at the top to orange (about #E86A1A) at the bottom. Two hues — amber and orange — with the screen's dark end anchoring the composition.

**Typography.** There is no typography on the screen. The absence is the record: this image contributes a gradient material, not a type decision.

**Layout & harmony.** Single column, estimated by eye: the monitor is centred on a dark rock slab, the gradient wall fills the rest. Hierarchy is ranked by position and by the light fall alone — the silk highlight is the only focal point. Density is minimal — one object, one gradient, one wall.

**Shape & surface.** Estimated radius scale, by eye: the monitor is sharp-cornered with a thin black frame; the rock is an irregular slab. Separation is by tone — the dark screen against the pale wall — with no borders, no shadows inside the screen, no UI surfaces at all.

**Buttons & components.** There are no buttons and no components. The record exists for the gradient material and the staging, not for any interface element.

**Gradient & imagery.** The gradient is the entire content: an abstract amber silk wash, smooth, with no banding visible at this size. No photography, no illustration. It is a flat, cheap gradient — reproducible in CSS — and it is the only thing this image can teach.

**Contrast.** There is no text to score. The screen's own tonal range (near-black to pale gold) spans roughly 15:1 estimated between its darkest and lightest points, which is why the silk reads as a fold rather than a flat fill.

## Why the verdict

Adapt, low confidence: the amber silk gradient is a usable background material at low cost, but the image contains no UI whatsoever — it is a mockup staging shot, not a design reference — so its transferable value is one gradient, and the verdict is adapt only for that material.
