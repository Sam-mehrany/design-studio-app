# REF-0049 — Orange-and-black tracked infantry fighting vehicle — 3D render in white hangar

## Read

Values below were **measured by sampling pixels** (hex values from sampled points); there is no typography in the image. One line of context: a 3D render of a tracked armoured vehicle staged in a white hangar — an imagery / mood reference for game assets, not a UI screen. No UI archetype applies.

**Colour.** Measured: orange armour sampled at #D05102 against near-black hull panels and a white hangar field (#E0E1E5 dominant). Two hues only — vivid orange as the signal colour on a black/white field — so the orange reads as accent and identifier, not camouflage. No semantic colour beyond that split.

**Composition & form.** The vehicle fills roughly the middle two-thirds of the frame, three-quarter front view, cannon pointing left, turret mass centred slightly right of frame. Hierarchy is by mass and colour: the orange turret and side panels lead, the black tracks anchor the bottom. The form is all hard angles and flat panel breaks; no curves except the road wheels.

**Light & surface.** Soft top light from the hangar's strip lighting, with a glossy floor reflection doubling the vehicle. Surfaces are polished and hard-edged: flat armour plates with visible rivets and panel seams, gloss highlights on the orange, matte black on the tracks. Depth comes from the render's shading, not from any frame or border.

**Typography.** None — no legible type anywhere in the image.

**Contrast.** Measured: the orange/black split is high-contrast by construction (vivid orange #D05102 against black hull), and the dark vehicle against the white hangar field (#E0E1E5) is stark. Nothing here is text, so no WCAG pair applies; the contrast claim is about figure/ground separation only.

## Why the verdict

Adapt: a strong colour-and-form study — one vivid accent on a black/white field, hard-panelled form, clean studio staging — but it is a concept render, so it can only be adapted as mood/form reference, never adopted as a shipped pattern.
