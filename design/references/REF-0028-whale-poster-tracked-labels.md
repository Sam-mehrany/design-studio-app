# REF-0028 — "Make a poster every day" #184 — whale over lake, pink outline frame

## Read

*All hex values and contrast ratios below are **measured** with Python/PIL off the image (region medians, median-cut clusters, or darkest/lightest-5% glyph samples; WCAG 2.x formula). Type sizes and ratios are estimated by eye.*

**Colour.** Greyscale photograph (ground `#f4f4f4` → `#dedede` sky, `#2f2f2f` foreground) with one accent: a pink outline rectangle `#f786ad`, also echoed as its reflection in the lake. The accent is the only hue.

**Typography.** Every label is the same small, tracked, bold geometric sans in caps ("BIRTHDAY", "MAKE A POSTER EVERY DAY", "BY MAGDIEL LOPEZ"), set at the four margins — two rotated 90° on the sides, number "184" top-right. Essentially one size; hierarchy is by position, not scale.

**Layout & harmony.** Centred subject (whale tail) on a full-bleed landscape; the pink frame interlocks with it — tail passes in front of one edge and behind another — creating depth with a single stroke.

**Shape & surface.** Sharp-cornered rectangle at a medium stroke; no other shapes.

**Gradient & imagery.** Photo collage carries the piece; remove it and only the frame and four labels remain.

**Contrast.** Labels: "BIRTHDAY" `#222222` on `#dddddd` = 13.59:1; "184" ≈ 19.8:1; "BY MAGDIEL LOPEZ" light on `#343434` foreground = 14.95:1 (extremes). Pink `#f786ad` on sky `#dedede` = 1.74:1 — fine as decoration, would **fail** as a UI boundary (3:1 non-text).

## Why the verdict

The mechanism — micro-labels pinned to all four margins plus one accent outline interlocking with the subject — transfers to hero/cover design; the photo collage (imagery_dep hard) does not.
