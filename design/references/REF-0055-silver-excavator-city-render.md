# REF-0055 — Silver-grey futuristic excavator with gold trim against city skyline — 3D render

## Read

Values below were **measured by sampling pixels** (hex values from sampled points). One line of context: a 3D render of a futuristic excavator — silver-grey body, gold trim lines, glass cab — against a hazy city skyline at dusk. No UI archetype applies.

**Colour.** Measured: silver-grey body (#B4BFC5 dominant) with dark grey-black structure and thin gold trim lines as the single accent — gold is used only as pinstripes and small fittings, never as a field. The skyline behind is pale grey-blue haze, so the machine sits in the middle of the value range rather than popping against it. Muted saturation throughout except the gold.

**Composition & form.** Three-quarter view, arm rising steeply to the upper right and the bucket hanging at the right edge — the arm's diagonal mirrors REF-0054's but reversed, and the machine is shot from the opposite side. The body is smooth and rounded like REF-0054's, but the stance is wider and lower, and the skyline towers behind give the machine an urban scale the stone backdrop of REF-0054 does not.

**Light & surface.** Dusk light, soft and cool, with a warm gold light strip glowing on the rear body — the one warm light source in a cool image. Surfaces are polished silver with black glass and black hydraulic arms; the gold pinstripes catch the light as thin highlights along the arm and track edges.

**Typography.** Effectively none — small dark glyphs on the arm are illegible at this resolution and carry no readable message; a small gold badge on the arm is a mark, not type.

**Contrast.** Measured: dark tracks and arm structure against the pale hazy sky are moderate-to-high separation, but the silver body against the pale sky is low separation — the machine's upper edges dissolve into the haze. The gold trim is the only high-contrast detail, and it is thin. No text, so no WCAG pair applies.

## Why the verdict

Adapt: the gold-as-pinstripe accent system is the transferable lesson — a single warm accent used only at edges on a cool silver field — but a concept render, so adapt as mood/form reference only.
