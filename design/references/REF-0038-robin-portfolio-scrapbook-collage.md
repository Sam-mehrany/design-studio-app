# REF-0038 — Robin — product-designer portfolio as a 10-screen scrapbook collage

## Read

Values below are **estimated by eye** — the image is a 10-screen contact sheet, so pixel samples are not meaningful per screen; type sizes are estimated by eye and labelled as such. One line of context: a product designer's portfolio site shown as ten screens on a grey grid — pixel type, sticky-note highlights, taped photos, case-study cards ("Wayline", "Tandem", "Forge") and a "LET'S TALK" contact screen.

**Colour.** Estimated by eye: white lined-paper screens (about #F5F5F0) with black pixel type and five highlighter hues — yellow (about #F5D90A), pink (about #FF7FB0), green (about #3DD68C), blue (about #4A90E2) and red. Polychrome: the highlights are categorical (each tag colour marks a skill or a project), not decorative — yellow for nav, pink/green/blue for skill tags.

**Typography.** Estimated by eye: display headings ("ROBIN", "FEATURED WORKS", "WAYLINE", "ABOUT", "CONTACT") in a pixel/display face at about 36–48 px, body in a mono/grotesque at about 11–13 px, micro-labels at about 8–9 px. Dramatic scale — the pixel headings are roughly 4× the body — with two faces (pixel display + mono body) as the site's voice.

**Layout & harmony.** Modular grid, estimated by eye: ten screens in a 2×5 contact sheet on a grey grid background; each screen is itself a loose collage — taped photos, sticky notes, rotated labels, hand-drawn stars. Hierarchy is ranked by size (pixel headings first), then by the highlight colours. Density is high across the sheet, low within any one screen.

**Shape & surface.** Estimated radius scale, by eye: screens sharp-cornered, sticky notes and tags at about 2–4 px radius, photos with a white tape border. Separation is by the paper's ruled lines and by the highlight blocks — no shadows, no cards with elevation; the collage is flat.

**Buttons & components.** The components are the nav pills (yellow "HOME" active), the skill tags (Interaction Design, Prototyping, User Research, Motion Design), the case-study cards with dates and thumbnails, and the "LET'S TALK" contact screen with a smiley. The tags are the loudest UI element after the headings — colour-coded chips that carry the site's information architecture.

**Gradient & imagery.** No gradient. Photographs appear as taped Polaroid-style prints inside the collage — a Macintosh, a desk, project screenshots. Delete them and the site still reads (type, tags and layout survive), so the dependency is soft; the photos are texture, not structure.

**Contrast.** Estimated by eye: black pixel type on white paper is about 17:1 — passes. Black type on the yellow highlight, estimated at about #F5D90A (about 12:1 estimated — passes, estimated, not measured). Black on the pink highlight, estimated at about #FF7FB0 (about 6:1 estimated — passes, estimated, not measured). The smallest rotated micro-labels at 8 px are the most fragile text.

## Why the verdict

Adapt: the pixel-type + highlighter-tag + taped-photo scrapbook system is a transferable portfolio voice at low cost, but it is a concept contact sheet (ten screens at thumbnail size, no single screen legible in full) and the collage depends on careful art direction to stay readable, so adapt, not adopt.
