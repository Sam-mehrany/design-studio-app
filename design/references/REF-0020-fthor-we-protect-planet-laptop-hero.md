# REF-0020 — Fthor — "We Protect Not Just People, We Protect The Planet" editorial laptop hero

## Read

Values below are **estimated by eye** — pixel samples landed on the dark staging background, not the screen, so no hex is claimed as measured. One line of context: a law/ESG firm's landing hero on a laptop in a dark studio — a giant mixed-type headline wrapped around a photograph of a fountain pen holding a green sprig.

**Colour.** Estimated by eye: screen near-white #F5F5F2, black type, one muted sage green (about #8A9B7E) in the pill CTA and the leaves. Monochrome plus one quiet green: the green marks the living subject (the plant) and the single CTA, nothing else.

**Typography.** Estimated by eye: headline at about 64–72 px mixing three voices — a didone caps ("WE PROTECT"), a heavy grotesque ("NOT JUST", "PLANET.") and an italic script ("People", "The"). Dramatic scale: the headline is roughly 6× the 11–12 px body and nav. The script words are the emphasis device, set inside the caps lines.

**Layout & harmony.** Canvas layout, estimated by eye: the pen photograph runs vertically down the centre and the headline lines overlap it left and right, with a small vertical nav top-centre, a right-column standfirst and a bottom-left intro paragraph. Hierarchy is ranked by size, then by the script's contrast against the caps. Density is low — most of the screen is white space around one image and one headline.

**Shape & surface.** Estimated radius scale, by eye: the pill CTA and the small round arrow button are the only curves; everything else is sharp. Separation is by overlap, not by border or shadow — type sits directly on the photograph, and a faint dashed circle arcs behind the composition. No cards, no elevation.

**Buttons & components.** Two controls: a sage pill ("book a consultation") bottom-left and a black pill ("get contact") top-right, plus a small round black arrow button beside the CTA. The CTAs are quiet relative to the headline — the type is the action here, the buttons are secondary.

**Gradient & imagery.** No gradient. One photograph — the pen with the sprig — is the centre of the composition and the headline is written around it. Remove it and the headline still reads, but the overlap trick that makes the page collapses, so the dependency is soft, bordering on hard for the layout idea.

**Contrast.** Estimated by eye: black on near-white is about 17:1 — passes. The small grey standfirst, estimated at about #777777 on near-white (about 4.5:1 estimated — borderline, estimated, not measured). White type over the dark pen barrel, where it overlaps, is high contrast; black type over the pale leaf area is the one place the overlap risks legibility.

## Why the verdict

Adapt: the three-voice headline (didone caps + grotesque + script) wrapped around a single vertical photograph is a strong editorial mechanism at medium cost, but it is a staged concept mockup and the overlap depends on one specific photograph, so adapt, not adopt.
